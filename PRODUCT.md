# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Singapore secondary-school students learning practical AI literacy in a classroom setting. Many are first-time prompt writers and need clear orientation, low-pressure practice, and language that feels current without becoming confusing or patronising.

## Product Purpose

ProjectAI teaches students to work with AI by doing. Each quest turns an AI-literacy concept into a short scenario, lets the student try a prompt, shows what the agent produced, and coaches the student toward stronger judgment. Success means students understand why specificity, verification, privacy, and human oversight matter, not merely that they earn a high score.

## Brand Personality

Encouraging, playful, and candid. The coach should feel like a patient senior student: energetic enough to make practice fun, calm when a learner is stuck, and always clear about what the system is doing.

## Anti-references

Avoid dense walls of instructions, unexplained scoring, harsh failure language, noisy gamification, tiny labels, and chat screens where the next action is unclear. Do not make students guess whether the agent is thinking, building, evaluating, or waiting. Avoid hints that reveal a completed answer.

## Design Principles

1. Orient before asking: explain the goal, current step, and next action in plain language.
2. Coach with questions: hints should narrow attention and prompt thinking without writing the answer.
3. Show cause and effect: connect details in a prompt to changes in the produced artifact.
4. Make revision feel safe: retries are practice, and feedback should identify one manageable improvement at a time.
5. Keep challenge progressive: start with familiar school scenarios, reveal complexity in layers, and let confident learners move quickly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard-only use, visible focus, screen-reader labels, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, non-colour status cues, readable line lengths, and responsive layouts suitable for school laptops and student phones.
